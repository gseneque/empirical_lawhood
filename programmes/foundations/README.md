## Foundations synthesis studies

These five studies connect entropy production, predictive memory and response. They ask whether the same observations can distinguish forward paths from their physical reverses, predict later observations, and predict what an intervention will change. The circuit gives an exactly solvable example. The matrix and event studies measure how much predictive accuracy is lost when an observer discards information.

| Study | What the experiment established | Evidence and reproduction |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Matrix preparation and physical reversal | The hot preparation gave an entropy-production rate of 48.395 [47.256, 49.535] against an exact 48. Both preparation controls met tolerance across 4,096 evaluation roots. The 128-family driven cycle absorbed heat. Its endpoint entropy change was unknown, and the path comparison left positive dissipation unresolved. | [Results](emergent-structure/records/matrix-path-thermodynamics.md) · [Run guide](emergent-structure/workflows/matrix-path-thermodynamics/guide.md) |
| 2. Matrix orientation and signed response | On 24 held-out families, hiding the global orientation cost 0.00327 [0.00228, 0.00424] pulse-displacement units RMS at tick 64, within the 0.01 margin. The fitted predictor given the orientation had higher error than the zero forecast. At tick 128 the interval crossed the margin. This pilot tested the receiver alone. | [Results](emergent-structure/records/matrix-orientation-receiver.md) · [Run guide](emergent-structure/workflows/matrix-orientation-response/guide.md) |
| 3. Matrix memory and a blind two-pulse forecast | On 128 held-out preparations, one earlier position sample reduced signed mean-square error from 0.22790 to 0.00001723 relative to present positions and drive. A drive-only average already met the signed tolerance on both pulse recipes. All 14 field models missed at least one requirement for the complete trajectory. | [Results](emergent-structure/records/matrix-projected-memory.md) · [Run guide](emergent-structure/workflows/matrix-projected-memory/guide.md) |
| 4. Thermal circuit and voltage memory | The driven voltage preserved the exact forward/reverse likelihood ratio. For two grounded branches with unequal relaxation rates, their summed voltage retained predictive memory (lag-two conditional covariance 0.07969 [0.06935, 0.09003]). The equal-rate control agreed with zero. Sampling resolved a positive dissipation rate at the driven phase. Equivalence to the exact rate remained open. | [Results](measurement-and-response/records/thermal-rc-dissipation.md) · [Run guide](measurement-and-response/workflows/thermal-rc/guide.md) |
| 5. Marked events and later counts | Across 4,096 held-out histories, retaining only the clock or only the last site gave lower bounds of 0.03585 and 0.01448 count per active event on RMS loss. Both exceeded the 0.01 margin. Three richer summaries were inconclusive. Full history replay recovered the conditional response to roundoff, and signed effects largely cancelled in the aggregate. | [Results](measurement-and-response/records/marked-event-response.md) · [Run guide](measurement-and-response/workflows/marked-event-response/guide.md) |

For EL, a reusable result specifies the preparation, allowed intervention, quantity predicted, horizon and error tolerance. Study 3 shows why the baseline belongs in that statement. Study 5 shows why an accurate average can still hide a consequential error for an individual event. All assigned evaluation units contributed across seven numerical runs with distinct development and evaluation cohorts. Protected confirmation and transfer to other systems remain open.
