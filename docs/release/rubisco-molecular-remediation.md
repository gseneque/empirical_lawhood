## 2. Failure chain and why the original acceptance was insufficient

The molecular diagnostic report describes an exact reproduction of the original custom heavy-atom builder, including its 70,272-pose search. For LSU Gln11 in chains A and G, all candidate poses had a heavy contact below 1.6 Å; the builder selected the best score without a separate feasibility refusal. The reported all-four-material common LSU construction propagated the defect. Amber subsequently added hydrogens and warned about an approximately 0.349 Å Ser9–Arg15 contact. A valid parameterized topology therefore did not establish a physically acceptable all-atom structure.

The five original I95M returns in that report had a final maximum Cartesian gradient of approximately 123,247 kJ/mol/nm against a selected limit of 1. The reported whole-system contacts below 1 Å decreased from 131 to 16, while bonds differing from equilibrium by more than 0.15 Å increased from 3 to 263 and aggregate bond-strain energy rose from approximately 13,469 to 154,161 kJ/mol. The final reported C–H distance was approximately 0.533 Å against an equilibrium distance near 1.09 Å. Its plane-deviation diagnostic above 0.1 Å increased from 40 to 104. These observations explain why falling total energy and improving a few local distances cannot certify the remaining structure.

The audit distinguishes several failures: construction feasibility, inadequate whole-molecule geometry coverage, optimizer convergence, a returned-artifact publication limit, and reconstruction of a derived curvature scalar. A few-ULP BLAS-dependent scalar discrepancy cannot explain forces already enormous in the original retained arrays. The report’s high-precision local LJ/bond derivative checks support those selected diagnostic formulas, not every term in the coupled periodic QM/MM operator.

The 0.15 Å, 0.1 Å and 1 Å retrospective bins in the diagnostic report are descriptive diagnostics. They must not be relabelled as prospectively selected acceptance standards. New thresholds, source identities and budgets belong to a new experiment; the original five returns remain original evidence with their original failed or unresolved dispositions.

## 3. Revised construction and preparation ownership

The default preparation path uses established library implementations at the native boundary: PDBFixer for selected missing heavy atoms and all-eight I95M side chains; Amber/LEaP for explicit residue templates, hydrogen placement, solvation and parameter assignment; OpenMM's complete Amber force implementation for molecular minimization. PDBFixer is not permitted to replace KCX or remove RuBP, Mg, waters or other retained heterogens through generic cleanup defaults. It must preserve protected source anchors, complete atom/residue identities and the authored mutation census. Its documentation describes missing-atom/residue and hydrogen operations; EL must still choose which operations are chemically appropriate [1].

Both PDBFixer completion and mutation explicitly select OpenMM's Reference platform; their construction seed is part of the canonical source selection and public preparation interface. Complete Amber construction minimization also uses Reference. Thus construction does not silently choose an available GPU. Later periodic electronic work still selects its declared local CPU or accelerated engine according to the computational workload; neither backend grants additional scientific admission.

AmberTools provides preparation and parameterization infrastructure, including LEaP, ParmEd and tools for organic molecules and metal sites [2]. The currently selected ff14SB/TIP3P/GAFF2-related construction stack and retained KCX/RuBP parameter inputs must be captured exactly. ff14SB is a published protein force field [3]; it does not automatically validate carbamylated lysine, ligand protonation or Mg coordination. Moving to ff19SB/OPC would be a separately selected physical/numerical model, not a silent upgrade or a cure for malformed geometry [4].

The retained source stock is `ff14sb.frcmod` (Amber `frcmod.ff14SB`), `parm10.dat`, `kcx.prepin`, `kcx.frcmod`, `rubp.prepi` and `rubp.frcmod`, loaded with `leaprc.protein.ff14SB`, `leaprc.water.tip3p` and `leaprc.gaff2` plus the selected KCX aliases/boundary coefficients. Initial construction uses HIE, eight KCX, eight Mg, eight RuBP, the 1,084 original waters, a 10 Å TIP3PBOX solvent buffer and neutralizing Na/Cl. These are explicit model choices, not proof that every site/protonation or solvent condition is physically adequate. The new construction stock requires hashes of the selected `pdbfixer`, `openmm`, `OpenMM.libs`, `numpy`, `numpy.libs`, `scipy` and `scipy.libs` trees and interpreter. Actual installed package versions were not available for native qualification in this review. A fresh native run must authenticate the installed versions.

OpenMM and ParmEd can consume Amber topology and coordinate files directly [5,6]. Every handoff must nevertheless verify atom order, parameters, bonded terms, exclusions, 1–4 scaling, masses, periodic cell, nonbonded convention and coordinate correspondence. The library boundary removes a need to substitute a custom projected pair potential for complete Amber minimization. It does not eliminate EL's obligation to check the composition or guarantee parameter adequacy for the chosen chemistry.

This replacement applies to molecular construction and its complete Amber minimization. The coupled QM/MM prefix and relaxation still use the explicit `geometry_domain_minimizer` through `active_preparation_native` / `active_relaxation_native`; they require their own independent optimization, admissible-domain, continuation and complete-force controls. The library construction change does not certify that custom optimizer or turn a finite prefix into a converged state. Revalidate its geometry guard at every selected trial and its independently read final or checkpoint state.

## 4. Acceptance standards: literature, derivation and authored policy

These are three different kinds of standard. A literature convention identifies a measurement; a derived inequality specifies what an observation can imply; an authored threshold allocates acceptable error for this experiment. A threshold is not validated merely because it resembles a number used in a paper.

| Measurement or gate | Standard and application | What it cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All-atom steric contacts | Actual MolProbity/Reduce/Probe-style validation uses approximately 0.4 Å nonbonded van der Waals overlap, with hydrogen placement and the tool's bonding/donor–acceptor rules [7]. Retain tool version and atom/template coverage. | A center-to-center cutoff of 1 Å or 0.6 Å is not a MolProbity clashscore. Unknown ligand hydrogens cannot be treated as complete coverage. |
| Bond and angle geometry | MolProbity reports deviations relative to empirical reference geometry; its conventional outlier criterion is approximately four reference standard deviations [7]. Use chemically appropriate definitions for standard residues and separately qualified ligand/metal chemistry. | A force-field harmonic thermal scale is not an empirical crystallographic geometry standard deviation. |
| Gross all-atom and bonded screen | The new typed EL policy initially authors a 1.00 Å nonbonded-distance refusal for all atoms, with an explicit heavy-atom floor also at 1.00 Å, and a separate absolute 0.60 Å nuclear-distance floor including bonded/QM pairs. It adds 0.20 Å bond-deviation and 30° angle-deviation ceilings plus local harmonic strain screening. The exact serialized policy controls execution. | These are conservative defect screens, not universally validated chemical-quality thresholds. Passing is labelled `SCREEN_PASSED`, not chemical or ensemble admission. |
| Local harmonic strain | For OpenMM convention \(E=k(q-q_0)^2/2\), define \(z=|q-q_0|\sqrt{k/(RT)}\). The initial authored ceiling is six. Amber coefficients require the factor-of-two and Å/nm/kcal/kJ conversions before applying this expression. | Coupled protein coordinates are not independent Gaussian samples. The ceiling is not a six-sigma physical reliability guarantee. |
| Improper/planarity and proper torsions | Preserve source improper metadata and apply any authored plane criterion only to terms whose selected equilibrium is planar, accounting for signed force coefficients and phase. | Construction stereochemistry | Independently check the full-CIP sign of all 16 RuBP stereocentres before and after minimization and the final protein stereocentre census. The external geometry report remains necessary for scientific admission after the internal gross screen. It must cover hydrogens and the actual biological assembly, not only one catalytic core. Record unsupported residue/ligand templates as missing coverage, not a zero-outlier result. The numerical gate must be assessed at each consequential composition, because valid parts can still be combined with inconsistent units, masks, atom order, exclusions, periodic images or electronic states.

Construction geometry summaries are checked against independent raw-coordinate calculations. Policy, atom/term census, findings and disposition remain exact; explicitly bounded floating-point diagnostic differences cannot cross an acceptance boundary. This permits harmless last-bit singular-value-decomposition variation without accepting a changed scientific verdict. The final electronic residual is also required to be finite before differentiation: a converged SCF flag cannot turn a NaN residual into an admitted force.

A transferred QM proton can create a covalent bond absent from the original Amber graph. Its declared 0.98 Å O–H distance must not be misclassified by the 1.00 Å nonbonded screen. For those alternatives, author exact `MolecularGeometryPolicy.reactive_hydrogen_bond_pairs` from the selected chemical graph and complete source atom mapping before preparation; the default is empty and the frozen M50 keto case needs none. Each listed pair must contain exactly one hydrogen and both atoms must be in the actual selected QM population. Only those pairs receive the 0.60 Å absolute nuclear floor, so a 0.533 Å collapse still refuses. All other contacts retain their normal criteria. The complete canonical policy and selected-pair census are independently retained and assessed. The [preparation workflow](../../programmes/biology/photosynthesis/workflows/rubisco-response/preparation-response-v1.md#prospectively-declared-transferred-protons) gives the explicit graph-based authoring procedure; bonds are never inferred from a convenient observed distance.

`PeriodicProbeReadout.geometry_quality` now retains an independent assessment reconstructed from the complete authenticated source topology and actual coordinate array, including a coordinate fingerprint, source identity, atom/term census, metrics, policy and explicit wholly-QM bonded exemptions. The QM exemption applies to MM equilibrium bond/angle/torsion terms, not gross nuclear contacts. Probe, control, action and M50 response readers compose this check with their existing source/coordinate/log authentication. Missing or unsupported source terms cannot produce `SCREEN_PASSED`; rejected or unevaluable geometry cannot supply the force operands used by the downstream numerical comparison. This checks the original bytes rather than trusting a producer's summary. The exact serialized, relaxed Amber restart is independently bound to downstream representation coordinates and is re-evaluated by the complete native force before a construction result can claim convergence.

## 6. New prospective numerical-refinement admission

The molecular diagnostic report records prior WT controls with grid/direct-embedding maximum-gradient changes near 18.7/1.93 kJ/mol/nm but no originally selected refinement-accuracy thresholds. Those observations do not retroactively acquire a pass/fail criterion. This audit found the same software gap in the candidate: refinement differences were retained, but the readout aggregated only directional comparisons.

The corrected API requires every new `PeriodicNumericalRefinement` to carry a `PeriodicRefinementBudget`. The budget requires an explicit maximum absolute Cartesian gradient-component change in Hartree/Bohr and a written justification. Optional criteria bound RMS over all 3N Cartesian components and absolute energy change in Hartree. A protocol explicitly authors `required_refinement_kinds`; an omitted required kind refuses selection before native entry. Every selected refinement is included in numerical assessment, whether or not its kind also appears in that required-kind set.

The readout now retains three separate results:

| Field | Proposition |
| --- | --- |
| `directional_disposition` | All selected directional derivative and step-change criteria are met. |
| `refinement_disposition` | All selected same-geometry numerical refinements meet their authored sensitivity budgets. With no refinement selected, this proposition remains unevaluable. |
| `numerical_disposition` | Every selected directional and refinement question is satisfied. Any selected failure falsifies this proposition; a missing required return leaves it unevaluable unless another selected failure already falsifies it. |

The aggregate is limited to the selected numerical questions. It never asserts physical-model adequacy or an enclosure of the exact force. The budget, refinement, protocol and readout identities bind the criteria prospectively. Changed canonical contracts use version 2.0.0; old unbudgeted refinement records are rejected rather than silently upgraded. The force-action protocol also changes canonical revision because it shares the stricter protocol field set.

The separate `read_periodic_molecular_precision` diagnostic still reports authenticated same-geometry CUDA/Reference differences as `COMPARED`. It does not issue an accuracy pass. Declare the platform-comparison error budget before collecting and using those new observations. A successful directional/refinement readout must not be substituted for that additional selected native-platform requirement or for M50's explicitly unavailable force-error enclosure.

### Receiver-based budget derivation

For a fixed receiver vector \(r\) and an observed force-refinement difference \(\Delta g\), Hölder's inequality gives

\[
|r^T\Delta g|\le \|r\|_1\|\Delta g\|_\infty.
\]

If the researcher allocates a fraction \(f\) of useful receiver error \(\varepsilon_R\) to one projected-force sensitivity, a sufficient component criterion is

\[
\|\Delta g\|_\infty\le f\varepsilon_R/\|r\|_1.
\]

`prepare_receiver_refinement_budget` records the receiver identity, useful error, fraction and supplied dimensionless L1 norm. Its arithmetic rounds the positive allowance down, validates the complete derivation, and does not invent a universal default. The L1 norm must be computed from the actual frozen receiver; using its unit Euclidean norm as a substitute is incorrect. A whole-enzyme RMS can hide one bad component, so it does not replace the maximum-component gate.

For an action \(Hu\) estimated by central gradient differences at step \(h\), independent force error bounded by \(\varepsilon_g\) in Euclidean norm at both endpoints contributes at most \(\varepsilon_g/h\) to action error. A unit-receiver forecast at amplitude \(\alpha\) then incurs at most \(\alpha\varepsilon_g/h\). The two forces in an observed response difference contribute at most \(2\varepsilon_g\), giving a conservative residual contribution \((2+\alpha/h)\varepsilon_g\), before finite-difference truncation and model error. These are **conditional derived bounds**; observing one numerical refinement does not establish their force-error premise. The convenience helper only budgets one projected force difference, not this multi-operand residual.

For illustration, the authored M50 defaults of 0.1 RT work-equivalent useful error over a 0.2-Bohr challenge at 298.15 K correspond to approximately 23.423 kJ/mol/nm of receiver force error. A numerical fraction of 0.1 allocates approximately 2.342 kJ/mol/nm to the selected numerical contribution. With \(h=0.01\) Bohr, the conditional residual formula above would allocate approximately 0.1065 kJ/mol/nm of full gradient Euclidean error if that entire numerical allowance were assigned to those force terms. These values are derived from this design, not literature thresholds, measured uncertainty or an activation-energy bound. New experiment authors must state which quantity they budget and include all propagation factors.

## 7. Researcher authoring and execution order

Work backward from the receiver before purchasing calculations. Bind the physical material/processing alternative, permitted preparation, information cutoff, interventions, fixed receiver/frame, finite horizon, useful error, costs and intended falsifier. Then choose the smallest set of source/structure, force, response and downstream suppliers needed to answer that scoped question. Composing known modules does not waive the validity contract at a connection.

1. **Author a fresh WT/I95M construction selection.** Preserve the assembly, all-eight mutation, M1/V1 distinction, input source bytes and processing/protonation choices. Capture actual library/template versions. Do not relabel original problematic constructions or re-enter their already completed native contacts.
2. **Complete heavy atoms and mutation through the library path; build Amber topology and hydrogens.** Preserve warnings. Perform all-atom geometry checks immediately after hydrogen placement, before a costly quantum contact. The supplied audit's short H–H contacts must be regression cases.
3. **Minimize and independently assess the complete molecular system.** Retain input/output coordinates, all-force components, mobile/fixed scope, energy decomposition, converged versus work-limited status, internal screen and external structural-quality report. Refuse missing parameters, gross contacts, damaged bonded geometry and incomplete quality coverage.
4. **Qualify the exact coupled operator for its intended use.** Inspect QM/MM cuts, caps, charges, self/images and complete force decomposition. Author finite derivative tests and required refinement kinds with receiver-specific budgets before observing new outputs. Use small CPU controls where they test the same calculation; qualify native CPU/GPU equivalence on the selected workload. An explicitly numerical sensitivity pass does not close chemical-model requirements.
